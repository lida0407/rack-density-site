The Landover Mall data center, a $5 billion campus planned at Brightseat Park in Prince George's County, Maryland, is stalled. In October 2026 the County Council passed a bill that sends the application back for a council vote, closing a 2021 shortcut, WTOP reported. A county moratorium and a petition with more than 25,000 signatures came first.

## 1. What changed in Prince George's County

[WTOP reported in October 2026](https://wtop.com/maryland/2026/10/prince-georges-co-bill-may-pull-the-plug-on-data-center-at-former-landover-mall-site/) that the county council had voted in 2021 to take itself out of the approvals process for data centers, to speed construction. Council member Shayla Adams Stafford, whose district includes the site, called that a loophole that allowed the Brightseat Park development to go forward. The new law requires the application to go before the council again, which she said essentially kills it given the opposition.

The same bill changes the process for every future application: detailed site plans that consider how surrounding land is used, a more in-depth public review, and a council say on each project. WTOP says the legislation was a response to opposition to the Landover project, which led to the county's first data center moratorium in 2025. Council members said at the time that they had been unaware of the plans until it was almost too late.

[WJLA 7News](https://wjla.com/news/local/landover-data-center-prince-georges-county-brightseat-park-mall-maryland-development-opposition-shayla-adams-chesapeake-climate-council-vote-data-center-moratorium) (2 November 2026, updated 9 November) adds that the council vote came roughly three months after county leaders extended the moratorium. Beth Landry of the CCAN Action Fund told the station the project should follow any new building standards, if and when the moratorium is lifted, instead of the old permitting rules. WJLA's own reading: the project is not officially dead, but its path forward is much harder.

## 2. The site and the timeline

| 2002 | Mall closes; the site stays mostly vacant | WJLA 7News, NPR |
| 2014 | Sears, the last major store, closes | WJLA 7News |

| 2026 | Moratorium extended; about three months later the council passes the bill | WJLA 7News, WTOP |

NPR describes nearly 90 acres of empty land, with the development able to occupy more than 80 acres. NPR also says the project had been set to break ground between 2026 and 2027.

## 3. What residents objected to

[NPR's profile of 7 November 2026](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/07/nx-s1-6007770/data-centers-activist-moratorium) follows Taylor Frazier McCollum, who lived less than a mile from the site when she heard about the plan. Her objection, in her words to NPR, was to "water-polluting, diesel-generating data centers right down the street from where people live, work, play." Her petition gathered more than 25,000 signatures and led to well attended protests and several local moratoriums.

Her coalition, NPR writes, mixed socialists, environmentalists, people angry about their electric bills and neighbors new to organizing. Staci Hartwell, a longtime local environmental activist, told NPR she had struggled for years to get traction on the issue. NPR puts the national count at about 500 data center moratoriums, while noting that estimates vary.

## 4. The revenue side

The county needs the money. NPR lists the pressures: a local Six Flags closed last year, the Washington Commanders plan to return to Washington, D.C., thousands of federal workers have lost their jobs, and the county faces a budget deficit. At a council meeting in September, council member Wanika Fisher, who supports moving some data center projects forward, said "Our county has an economic future that we have to grapple with" and that a data center is "worth the hard discussion."

Frazier McCollum has pitched a sports complex, affordable housing or heritage tourism for the site, and acknowledged to NPR that none would be as lucrative as a data center campus. The same tension between tax income and neighbors shows up in the [Kraft Group plan for Sutton, Massachusetts](/dc/resources/guides/kraft-group-sutton-data-center/).

## 5. What site planners can take from Landover

These points are our reading of the case, not findings from the reporting.

- An approval granted under a fast track can be reopened. Check how a jurisdiction's approval path was created and whether a council can pull it back, as Prince George's did with its 2021 change.
- Distance to homes drives the reaction. At less than a mile from residents, generators and water use became the core complaints. Publish generator, noise, water and lighting plans before the first hearing, not after the petition.
- Budget for moratorium risk. The county moved from no moratorium to a moratorium, an extension and a new review law within about two years.
- A large vacant retail site looks like an easy win on paper. Here the acreage was there, but the neighbors were too.
