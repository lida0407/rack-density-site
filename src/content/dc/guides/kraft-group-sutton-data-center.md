The Kraft Group data center proposed for Sutton, Massachusetts, would grow from two buildings of about 90 MW each in 2029 to ten buildings and roughly 900 MW by 2033, with more than $16 billion of investment, according to a town memo reported by DCD on 11 November 2026. No application has been filed and the town would vote in May 2027.

## 1. What is proposed

[DCD reported on 11 November 2026](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/kraft-group-could-build-900mw-data-center-campus-in-sutton-massachusetts-investing-16-billion/) that Sutton town manager Austin Cyganiewicz set out the plan in a memo to the town's Select Board. The Kraft Group, the conglomerate owned by Robert Kraft, would develop the campus with Unified Global Packaging Group, a packaging firm based in Sutton.

The site is Unified's Parkway property, off Boston Road and Providence Road. The memo's figures, as DCD reported them:

| Item | Figure from the town memo |
|---|---|
| First phase | 2 buildings, planned to open in 2029 |
| Building size | about 250,000 sq ft (23,225 sq m) each |
| Power per first-phase building | roughly 90 MW |
| Build-out | 2 more buildings a year until 2033, 10 in total |
| Power at full build-out | around 900 MW |
| Investment | more than $16 billion, developer's estimate |
| Workloads | not disclosed |

Ten buildings at about 90 MW each is where the 900 MW figure comes from. It is a full build-out ambition five years out, not a grid connection anyone has signed.

## 2. Power and water: the open questions

The memo says details on power, water and equipment are still to come. Discussions are under way with the regional grid operator to secure the power, and the plan is to use reclaimed water for cooling.

That leaves the two things residents and planning boards ask about first without numbers. A campus of this size would need new transmission capacity and a substation sized for hundreds of megawatts, and its cooling design decides how much water it uses. Treated or reclaimed wastewater avoids drawing on drinking water, but the volume still has to be agreed with whoever supplies it. Our guide to [AI data center water use per day](/dc/resources/guides/ai-data-center-water-use-per-day/) explains why the cooling choice moves that figure so much.

For anyone planning power or cooling for a site like this, these are the documents to watch for once an application is filed: the grid operator's interconnection study, the reclaimed water agreement, the noise study and the equipment list for backup generation.

## 3. The tax argument

The memo puts the assessed value of all property in Sutton at $2.7 billion today. It estimates the campus would pay about $10.4 million a year in property tax once fully assessed in 2031, rising to $27.8 million at full build-out. The town's entire 2027 tax levy is projected at $32 million. Sutton had 9,357 residents in the 2020 census.

"Because the town raises only what its budget requires, the main effect would be lower tax bills," Cyganiewicz wrote, estimating about $2,300 less for the average single-family home in FY2031 and about $5,800 less in FY2035.

That is the case the developers and the town administration will put to residents. It is also why the project matters beyond Sutton: a single campus that pays almost as much tax as the whole town turns a zoning decision into a budget decision.

## 4. The approval path and local reaction

The proposal is "tentative" and no formal application has been filed. Cyganiewicz's timeline runs community benefits discussions, zoning bylaws and a technical review in parallel, ending with a town meeting and vote in May 2027.

State rules shape that path. Under an executive order from Massachusetts Governor Maura Healey, data centers over 25 MW must be approved by the local community, and the developer must sign a community benefits agreement that meets state standards.

The Select Board met on 10 November 2026. It was not a formal public hearing, but public comment was allowed. DCD, citing social media comments after the meeting, reported that residents raised concerns about noise from an operating data center and about a possible moratorium on data centers in the town.

The reaction fits the national pattern. DCD notes that several moratoria and other restrictions were voted on during the 3 November 2026 elections, which we covered in our guide to [data center ballot measures in Ohio](/dc/resources/guides/data-center-ballot-measures-ohio-2026/). In Europe, the same concerns drove the [Amsterdam data center protest](/dc/resources/guides/amsterdam-data-center-protest-europe/) a week earlier.

## 5. What to take from it

For site selectors, Sutton is a test of whether a large tax offer can carry a 900 MW campus through a town meeting in a state that requires local approval and a community benefits agreement. For utility and grid planners, it is a large load request in central Massachusetts with no published connection date yet. For operators and suppliers, nothing here is buildable before 2029, and the first firm numbers will arrive with the formal application.

The next dates to watch are the filing of that application and the May 2027 town meeting vote.
