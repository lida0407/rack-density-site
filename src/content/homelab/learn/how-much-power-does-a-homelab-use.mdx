## The rule

```
monthly kWh  = average watts × 730 ÷ 1,000
monthly cost = monthly kWh × your price per kWh
```

730 is the number of hours in an average month (8,760 ÷ 12). Average watts is what counts: a device that idles at 110 W and reaches 220 W for four hours a day averages about 128 W.

## Typical draw by device

These are the typical values the calculator below starts from; real devices vary, so measure yours at the wall when you can.

| Device | Idle (W) | Load (W) |
|---|---|---|
| Mini PC, low-power quad-core | 8 | 25 |
| Mini PC, 6–8 core | 12 | 50 |
| Raspberry Pi / single-board computer | 4 | 8 |
| NAS, 4 bays, disks spinning | 30 | 45 |
| Desktop tower used as a server | 35 | 120 |
| 1U server, older dual-socket | 80 | 160 |
| 2U server, older dual-socket | 110 | 220 |
| AI box with one 24 GB GPU | 60 | 400 |
| Switch, 8-port unmanaged | 4 | 5 |
| Switch, 24-port managed (PoE load not included) | 20 | 25 |
| Router / firewall appliance | 12 | 18 |

## Three example labs

Each device busy four hours a day and idle the rest; prices at €0.30 and $0.17 per kWh.

| Lab | Devices | Average | Per month |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small | 2 low-power mini PCs, 4-bay NAS, 8-port switch, router | ≈ 71 W | 52 kWh · €15.60 · $8.90 |
| Cluster | 3 mini PCs (6–8 core) in a Proxmox cluster, 4-bay NAS, 24-port switch, router | ≈ 121 W | 89 kWh · €26.60 · $15.10 |
| Used enterprise | 2 older 2U servers, 4-bay NAS, 24-port switch, router | ≈ 323 W | 236 kWh · €70.70 · $40.10 |

Adding an AI box with a 24 GB GPU to any of them adds about 117 W on average with the same four busy hours: another 85 kWh, about €25.60 or $14.50 a month.

## Where the watts go

- **Idle draw is most of the bill.** In the used-enterprise lab, close to 90 % of the energy is the idle draw that runs around the clock. Cutting idle watts (newer hardware, fewer always-on hosts, spinning down disks) saves more than tuning peaks; the [Proxmox power guide](/homelab/learn/proxmox-power-usage/) covers how.
- **Disks add up.** Every spinning 3.5-inch disk adds a few watts around the clock.
- **PoE is real power.** Cameras and access points powered by the switch show up in the switch's draw, not in their own.
- **The UPS has losses too,** and its runtime depends on the total: check it with the [UPS runtime calculator](/calculator/ups-runtime/).
